# Corporate & Foundation Relations — Standard Operating Procedure
## Complex Industry-Supported Research Gifts

*Draft v1.0 — informed by the PBM Capital / Manning Institute gift*

## Purpose

Provide a repeatable framework for evaluating, structuring, documenting, and stewarding complex industry gifts supporting research while protecting donor intent, institutional compliance, and the overall donor experience.

---

## Stage 1 — Discovery

**Objective:** Understand why the company wants to fund the project.

**Key Questions**
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- Why UVA?
- Why this faculty member?
- What does success look like?
- Is this philanthropy, research, commercialization, or procurement?
- What motivated them to reach out now?

**Deliverable:** Summary of donor intent.

---

## Stage 2 — Funding Pathway Assessment

**Determine the appropriate funding vehicle**

| Question | Gift | Sponsored Research | Contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unrestricted support | ✓ | | |
| Specific deliverables | | ✓ | ✓ |
| Publication restrictions | | ✓ | ✓ |
| Intellectual property expectations | | ✓ | ✓ |
| Donor directing research | | ✓ | ✓ |
| General stewardship only | ✓ | | |

**Discussion topics:** Tax implications, deliverables, statement of work, reporting, publication, IP, F&A, administrative fees, recognition.

**Decision point:** Determine preferred funding pathway.

---

## Stage 3 — Internal Stakeholder Mapping

**Assemble the team** (chain of engagement)

Corporate Relations → Development Officer → Department Leadership → Health Development (if applicable) → Office of Sponsored Programs → Gift Administration → Finance → Legal → Research Administration → Collaborating Institution

**Assign ownership:** Relationship lead, agreement lead, budget lead, compliance lead, accounting lead, stewardship lead.

---

## Stage 4 — Complexity Assessment

**Does the gift involve any of the following?**
- Multiple institutions
- Industry partner
- International entity
- Human subjects
- Export controls
- Intellectual property
- Existing equity ownership
- Conflict of interest
- Licensing
- Technology transfer
- Multiple funding entities
- Pass-through funding

**If yes: escalate immediately.**

---

## Stage 5 — Compliance Review

**Review chain:** Gift vs. Grant → COI → OSP → Legal → Gift Administration → Finance

**Questions**
- Does donor control research?
- Does donor receive deliverables?
- Does donor influence publication?
- Does donor receive IP?
- Is donor selecting collaborators?
- Will this remain philanthropic?

**Objective:** Protect donor intent while protecting institutional independence.

---

## Stage 6 — Documentation

**Possible documents:** Gift Agreement, Statement of Work, Memorandum, Budget, COI Forms, Subaward Agreement, Multi-party Agreement, Recognition Letter.

**Statement of Work**

*Purpose:* Describe scientific collaboration without creating sponsored research.

*Should include:* Research objectives, research themes, scientific scope, faculty collaboration, general use of funds.

*Should avoid:* Deliverables, milestones, deadlines, specific outcomes, sponsor direction, publication requirements.

---

## Stage 7 — Financial Review

**Review chain:** Administrative fees → Indirect costs (F&A) → Gift assessment → Budget → Payment timing → Account setup → Recognition

**Lesson learned:** Health Development applies a 5% administrative fee to gifts not designated to the general fund. Whenever appropriate, discuss whether the donor would consider covering this separately through an unrestricted gift — this preserves intended research dollars.

---

## Stage 8 — Multi-Institution Coordination

**If collaborating institutions exist, identify:** Receiving institution → Finance contact → Gift office → Research office → Agreement owner → Accounting workflow

**Questions:** Who receives funds? Who administers funds? Gift, subaward, or separate agreement? Who signs?

---

## Stage 9 — Accounting & Advancement

**Before funds arrive, confirm:** Hard credit → Soft credit → Campaign credit → Recognition → Acknowledgment letters → Stewardship plan

**Questions:** Who receives hard credit? Who receives soft credit? How should multi-party gifts appear in CRM? Can donor recognition reflect total philanthropic impact?

---

## Stage 10 — Stewardship

**Develop stewardship plan:** General research updates → Faculty engagement → Site visits → Recognition → Future opportunities

**Remember:** Stewardship ≠ Reporting. Avoid creating contractual obligations.

---

## Stage 11 — Relationship Expansion

**Once gift closes, identify:** Additional researchers → Centers → Corporate engagement → Consortium opportunities → Future proposals → Strategic partnership

**PBM example:** Research Gift → Manning Institute → BridgeBio → PBM Network → Potential $50M–100M future opportunity

| Collaborating Institution | Gift acceptance; local administration |

## Development Officer Best Practices

- Lead with donor intent.
- Educate, don't prescribe.
- Bring internal partners in early.
- Communicate often.
- Eliminate surprises.
- Think "One UVA."
- Protect the donor experience.
- Simplify complex university processes.
- Document every decision.
- Leave behind templates for the next project.

## PBM Capital — Lessons Learned

| Lesson | Future Standard |
|---|---|
| Donor intent changed over time. | Revisit donor motivation throughout the process. |
| OSP became a critical partner. | Engage OSP early for any research-related industry gift. |
| Multi-party gifts require special accounting. | Involve Gift Administration before agreements are finalized. |
| The Statement of Work clarified intent without creating obligations. | Develop a reusable SOW template for unrestricted research gifts. |
| The 5% Health Development fee reduced available research funding. | Discuss fee treatment early and consider separate unrestricted support when appropriate. |
| Stewardship expectations must remain philanthropic. | Separate stewardship from sponsored reporting requirements. |
| Multiple offices touched the project. | Assign a single CFR lead to quarterback communication and maintain a seamless donor experience. |

## Guiding Principle

Every complex industry gift should leave the University with a stronger process than it had before. Our role is not simply to secure funding — it is to align institutional partners, translate complexity for donors, protect the University's interests, and create an experience that makes industry partners want to work with UVA again.
